About

I like working where circuits meet code. That means describing a processor in VHDL and watching it execute instructions on an FPGA, or wiring sensors to an ESP32 and getting clean data out of them over Wi-Fi.

In my digital systems work I have designed and simulated finite state machines, registers, and ALUs in Quartus II, then implemented a small processor with an FSM controller, ALU, memory, and an instruction set on an Altera DE-series board. Writing the testbenches taught me more about timing than any lecture did.

On the embedded side I build things I can hold. My weather station runs eight-plus sensors off one ESP32 and streams to a live dashboard, with 3D-printed parts I modeled myself. Outside school I have built and tuned more than fifteen custom PCs, which is where I first learned to benchmark, diagnose, and fix hardware methodically.

Right now I am on the TMU Engineering Concrete Toboggan Team, programming a microcontroller-based interactive display for Canada's largest engineering competition.

ESP32 Weather Station

A weather station I designed, printed, wired, and programmed end to end. One ESP32 reads eight-plus sensors and streams the readings to a live dashboard over Wi-Fi.

When
June 2025
Role
Solo: mechanical, electronics, firmware
Stack
ESP32, C++ (Arduino), ThingSpeak, AutoCAD, FDM 3D printing

  1. Start with real weather

    I wanted a station that measured real weather, not just a temperature sensor on a desk. That meant wind speed and direction, rainfall, and pressure alongside the usual temperature, humidity, and light. Every instrument feeds a single ESP32. The moving ones use hall effect sensors and small magnets so nothing mechanical has to touch a switch.

  2. Design the parts that touch the weather

    The anemometer cups, wind vane, rain funnel, stacked radiation shield, and the electronics enclosure were all modeled in AutoCAD and printed on an FDM printer. The shield is a stack of louvred plates so the temperature sensor reads air, not sunlight.

  3. Assemble and revise

    Several parts took more than one revision before they fit and moved freely. The cups have to spin on the lightest breeze, the vane has to settle on a heading, and the funnel has to tip cleanly with a known volume of water.

  4. Stream it to a dashboard

    The firmware is C++ in the Arduino framework. It samples each sensor, converts raw values to units, and posts the set of fields to a ThingSpeak channel over Wi-Fi. ThingSpeak charts each field live, so I can watch a pressure drop or a gust from anywhere with a browser. Over a test window the data matched reference readings with better than 95 percent accuracy.

  5. Debug the whole system

    The hardest part was not any single sensor but getting all of them to behave on one board at the same time. I chased dropped readings and transmission errors back to power and wiring on the breadboard, and to timing between sensor polls and Wi-Fi posts. Fixing those is where I learned the most about hardware and software communication.

What each instrument measures

Temperature and humidityDigital sensor, polled on a fixed interval
Atmospheric pressureBarometric pressure sensor
LightLight sensor, charted as a raw level
Wind speedCup anemometer, hall effect pulse counting
Wind directionVane over an array of hall effect sensors, reported as N, E, S, W
RainfallPrinted funnel gauge, hall effect counting, reported in mm

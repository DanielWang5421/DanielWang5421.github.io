ESP32 Weather Station

A weather station I designed, printed, wired, and programmed end to end. One ESP32 reads eight-plus sensors and streams the readings to a live dashboard over Wi-Fi.

When
June 2025
Role
Solo: mechanical, electronics, firmware
Stack
ESP32, C++ (Arduino), ThingSpeak, AutoCAD, FDM 3D printing

Goal

I wanted a station that measured real weather, not just a temperature sensor on a desk. That meant wind speed and direction, rainfall, and pressure alongside the usual temperature, humidity, and light. It also meant the parts that touch the weather had to be designed and made by me.

Sensing

Every instrument feeds a single ESP32. The environmental sensors are read directly. The moving instruments use hall effect sensors and small magnets so nothing mechanical has to touch a switch.

Temperature and humidityDigital sensor, polled on a fixed interval
Atmospheric pressureBarometric pressure sensor
LightLight sensor, charted as a raw level
Wind speedCup anemometer, hall effect pulse counting
Wind directionVane over an array of hall effect sensors, reported as N, E, S, W
RainfallPrinted funnel gauge, hall effect counting, reported in mm

Mechanical

The anemometer cups, wind vane, rain funnel, stacked radiation shield, and the electronics enclosure were all modeled in AutoCAD and printed on an FDM printer. The radiation shield is a stack of louvred plates so the temperature sensor reads air, not sunlight. Several parts took more than one revision before they fit and moved freely.

Firmware and dashboard

The firmware is C++ written in the Arduino framework. It samples each sensor, converts raw values to units, and posts the set of fields to a ThingSpeak channel over Wi-Fi. ThingSpeak charts each field live, so I can watch a pressure drop or a gust from anywhere with a browser. Over a test window the collected data matched reference readings with better than 95 percent accuracy.

Debugging

The hardest part was not any single sensor but getting all of them to behave on one board at the same time. I chased dropped readings and transmission errors back to power and wiring issues on the breadboard, and to timing between sensor polls and Wi-Fi posts. Fixing those reduced transmission errors noticeably and is where I learned the most about hardware and software communication.

OSD32MP15x Carrier

A custom four-layer Linux single-board computer built around the Octavo Systems OSD32MP157C system-in-package. Derived from the vendor reference design and reduced to a targeted feature set on a 134.6 by 76.2 mm board, designed in KiCad 10.

Why a system-in-package

OSD32MP157C-512M-BAA Octavo Systems. 324-ball BGA, 1.0 mm pitch, 18 by 18 mm
  • STM32MP157C: dual Arm Cortex-A7 plus a Cortex-M4 coprocessor
  • 512 MB DDR3 SDRAM, routed inside the package
  • STPMIC1 power management and its passives, all integrated
  • 302 of the 324 balls are used on this board

Putting the DDR3 inside the package removes the hardest part of an STM32MP1 design from the carrier entirely: length-matched, impedance-controlled memory routing. What remains is a peripheral and power design, which is what made a four-layer board feasible at this density.

Reduced, not redrawn

The board began as the Octavo OSD32MP1-RED reference design and was cut down rather than drawn from scratch. Four subsystems came out: the three-phase motor-control interface with its two TXB0108 level-shifter banks, the DCMI camera port, the MIPI-DSI display channel, and the mikroBUS Click and 40-pin expansion headers. That took 19 components off the board, along with the analog sense chains and pull-ups that existed only to serve them.

Doing that carefully is most of the work. Level shifters had to be checked channel by channel, because the same SiP pins are muxed between the motor bank and the display bus, and deleting the wrong pair would have silently killed HDMI. The jumper that selects between those two banks had to stay, with its default position corrected, since a pulldown leaves the display path disabled unless it is fitted.

Provenance

Component placement and routing are derived from the Octavo reference design, which is published for this purpose. The engineering here is in the reduction: deciding what could be removed, verifying that nothing left behind depends on it, and confirming the result still routes and passes rules.

Layer stack

Signals run almost entirely on the outer layers, 7,040 of 7,105 track segments, which keeps both inner layers intact as reference planes. A solid ground plane sits directly beneath the BGA on In1, so every escaped signal has a continuous return path.

Putting the 4.7 µF and 100 nF decoupling on the back face, directly under the matching power balls, is what lets 129 of the 295 components live on the bottom side and keeps the top surface open for escape routing.

Every via is a plain through-hole placed between pads. No via-in-pad, no blind or buried vias, no filled-and-capped processing. The whole board sits inside standard fab capability, which was a deliberate constraint rather than an outcome.
